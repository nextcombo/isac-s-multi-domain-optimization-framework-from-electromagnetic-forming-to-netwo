ISAC's Multi-Domain Optimization Framework: From Electromagnetic Forming to Network Collaboration
Abstract Integrated Perception and Communications (ISAC) has become a key feature of the sixth-generation (6G) wireless network, offering the possibility to meet the dual demands of communication and perception. Existing ISAC research primarily focuses on baseband optimization for individual access points, with insufficient attention to the role of electromagnetic forming and network-wide collaboration. The complex interdependencies among domains have not been fully explored, and the full potential of enhancing ISAC performance has not been realized. To fill this gap, this paper proposes an ISAC multi-domain optimization method that integrates electromagnetic forming, baseband processing, and network collaboration strategies, enabling efficient resource management and system-level design. This paper analyzes the fundamental trade-off relationships between domains, elucidates domain-specific and cross-domain strategies that impact ISAC performance and efficiency, and subsequently validates the effectiveness of joint multi-domain optimization through case studies. Finally, this paper explores the critical challenges and future research directions connecting theoretical advancements with practical ISAC deployment. This study lays the foundation for an intelligent, scalable ISAC architecture and provides crucial insights for seamless integration into next-generation wireless networks.
Despite the aforementioned advantages, ISAC systems still face numerous challenges. The inherent characteristics of perception and wireless communication can impact system and waveform design: Communication generally requires moderate transmit power and stable data throughput, while perception needs higher power to detect distant or weakly reflected targets, which exacerbates self-interference in single-base station architectures; communication systems aim for low latency by favoring short symbol durations, for error correction by favoring long frames, while perception requires longer coherent intervals for precise velocity estimation, shorter pulses for distinguishing close returns, and typically wider bandwidth for high distance resolution. These differences complicate resource allocation, hardware design, and interference management, thus necessitating comprehensive optimization methods beyond traditional waveform central approaches.
The integrated design of ISAC systems must not compromise communication performance, ideally enhancing it through position information. Wireless resource sharing characteristics necessitate strict constraints. Common resource sharing methods include Time Division Duplex (TDD), Frequency Division Duplex (FDD), and Spatial Resource Partitioning. TDD simplifies hardware implementation but introduces self-interference due to rapid switching between transmission and reception, FDD naturally reduces interference by separating frequency bands but increases hardware complexity, and spatial resource partitioning assigns independent antennas or beams for different tasks to achieve high spectral efficiency, but faces high implementation complexity issues. Actual ISAC systems often balance performance tradeoffs by combining multiple methods.
While existing research has extensively explored the above resource sharing strategies, most focus on waveform and beamforming optimization at the baseband and radio frequency stages, optimizing signal characteristics such as amplitude, phase, frequency, and timing. However, they fail to fully consider the role of the electromagnetic domain—antenna array configuration determines the key characteristics of the radiated signals and array response. Due to the distinct propagation characteristics of radar targets and communication users, ignoring electromagnetic level optimization limits system flexibility. Therefore, dynamic antenna design and the control of radiation pattern with electromagnetic perception capabilities are essential conditions for fully leveraging ISAC capabilities.
In practical scenarios, the ISAC system also faces challenges such as clutter interference, weak target returns, and blurred feature extraction. In complex environments like low-altitude urban drone monitoring, these issues are even more pronounced: multipath reflections from buildings and vehicles produce dynamic clutter, while the echoes of small drones with low radar cross-sections approach the noise floor, limiting the performance of traditional detection and classification algorithms. Interference characteristics are also closely related to system configuration; single-baseline architecture inherently suffers from self-interference due to the co-location of transmit and receive arrays. Double-base or multi-baseline architectures mitigate interference through spatial separation, but this increases system complexity. Therefore, effectively canceling interference in multiple dimensions, even using advanced full-duplex technology, is crucial for achieving precise perception. Distributed or multi-baseline ISAC architectures alleviate self-interference through spatial separation and utilize spatial geometric diversity to achieve coherent fusion of signals from multiple perspectives, enhancing clutter suppression and target positioning accuracy, making them suitable for critical mission applications such as airborne surveillance and industrial automation.
To this end, this paper proposes a systematic multi-domain optimization framework that integrates electromagnetic shaping, baseband processing, and network collaboration. This framework enables cross-layer coordination from physical components to network-level operations. In the electromagnetic domain, flexible control over beam directional patterns and polarization enhances perception accuracy and communication throughput. The baseband processing domain focuses on waveform, beamforming optimization, and time-frequency code dimensions resource allocation. The network collaboration domain utilizes multi-node architecture, resource scheduling, and network perception processing to improve distributed ISAC systems' spatial diversity, adaptability, scalability, and collaborative performance. This paper will introduce relevant performance metrics and optimization strategies for each domain, and validate the effectiveness of joint multi-domain optimization through case studies. Finally, it will discuss future research directions and conclusions.
Method Introduction: The core method of this paper is to propose an ISAC multi-domain optimization framework that integrates electromagnetic domain, baseband processing domain, and network collaboration domain. By cross-layer coordination and targeted optimization in each domain, it addresses the performance trade-off issues of ISAC systems' perception and communication. At the same time, it achieves interference suppression, efficient resource utilization, and system performance enhancement. The core optimization methods in each domain and the overall verification method are as follows:
1  
Electromagnetic Field Optimization: Centered on reconfigurable antenna arrays as the core hardware foundation, we conduct optimization across three dimensions: radiation pattern, polarization, and array partitioning. This dynamic adjustment of antenna radiation characteristics adapts to the heterogeneous propagation characteristics of communication users and sensing targets. Simultaneously, we perform joint optimization of radiation pattern and polarization, considering hardware damages such as mutual coupling and amplitude-phase mismatch to achieve robust electromagnetic design. Based on electromagnetic information theory, we carry out theoretical performance analysis for channel capacity and the Cramer-Rao bound, providing a boundary reference for practical optimization.
2  
Baseband Processing Field Optimization: Resource allocation is conducted across three core dimensions of time, frequency, and code to adapt to the needs of low communication latency and long coherence interval perception, high distance resolution and high spectrum efficiency in communication, and multi-user interference resistance and waveform orthogonality. On this basis, multi-dimensional joint processing is carried out, achieving adaptive clutter suppression through space-time-frequency polarization diversity. High-resolution signal restoration is accomplished using techniques such as super-resolution and compressed sensing. By combining signal sparsity and low-rank characteristics, low complexity processing algorithms are designed to balance both perception accuracy and real-time implementation requirements.
3  
Network Collaboration Domain Optimization: Construct a multi-node collaboration architecture, optimize the dynamic allocation of access points (AP) roles and passive environmental shaping capabilities of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS), enhancing system spatial diversity and coverage range. Implement multi-node resource scheduling to adapt to simultaneous, frequency division duplexing (FDD)/time division duplexing (TDD) requirements for different duplex modes, and optimize handover management to ensure communication continuity and perception consistency. Through hierarchical collaborative information fusion at signal level, feature level, and decision level, address the spatiotemporal mismatch issues caused by node heterogeneity, improving target detection and positioning accuracy; ultimately achieve end-to-end joint optimization of the ISAC transmission and reception architecture, integrating graph neural networks, reinforcement learning, and other learning-based methods to enhance system dynamic adaptability.
4  
Multi-domain Joint Verification: Taking an unclustered MIMO-OFDM system equipped with a polarization reconfigurable antenna array as an example, setting up the scenario where distributed APs serve both communication users and detect radar targets simultaneously. The multi-domain optimization problem is decoupled into two subproblems: AP/subcarrier resource allocation and beamforming/polarization optimization, and an efficient solution algorithm is designed. By comparing with baseline schemes that only optimize in a single domain or partial domains, the effectiveness of multi-domain joint optimization is verified.
Conclusion This paper proposes a multi-domain optimization concept for enhancing the ISAC system performance through cross-domain coordination. It establishes an ISAC optimization research framework that integrates electromagnetic resource management, baseband processing, and network collaboration, providing clear guidance for independent domain optimization and cross-domain joint optimization research.
To drive the ISAC system from theoretical research to practical deployment, the core need is to address three key issues: scalability, security, and adaptability. In the future, the development of intelligent optimization algorithms and the formulation of related standards for ISAC will become important directions. The technological breakthroughs in these two directions are crucial for realizing the efficient and scalable operation of the ISAC system, integrating seamlessly into next-generation wireless networks, and providing core technical support for diverse application scenarios in 6G networks.
